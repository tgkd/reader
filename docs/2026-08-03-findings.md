# Findings — 2026-08-03

Four investigations that ran together: why the word-synced highlight drifted, why narration
moved off `eleven_v3`, why page OCR moved off `gemini-2.5-flash`, and why the reader now
reads furigana out of the book instead of only out of MeCab. A fifth thing surfaced at the
end — a playback bug that only the model change made visible.

Numbers here are measured, not estimated. Where something is inferred rather than observed
it says so, because the expensive mistakes this day produced were all confident inferences
that turned out wrong.

---

## 1. The highlight drift

**Symptom.** Audio said ユーфォニアム while the marker sat on 東京, in chapter 5 of a real
imported book. Reported before, "fixed" before, back again.

**Root cause.** `eleven_v3` speaks material that is not in the submitted text and returns no
timestamps for it. On the actual chapter (851 chars):

```
alignment ends   220.003 s
mp3 runs         227.680 s      (bytes ÷ 16000; mp3_44100_128 is CBR, so this is exact)
                 ───────
undescribed        7.68 s
```

Same text through `scripts/eleven-matrix.mjs`:

| model | route | undescribed |
|---|---|---|
| `eleven_v3` | stream | 9.64 s |
| `eleven_v3` | buffered | 6.94 s |
| `eleven_multilingual_v2` | stream / buffered | 0.04 / 0.07 s |
| `eleven_flash_v2_5` | stream / buffered | 0.04 / 0.07 s |
| `eleven_turbo_v2_5` | stream | 0.05 s |

`joinOK = true` and `backwards = 0` on every row — the alignment joins back to the text
exactly and never runs backwards. **No pre-existing check could see this.** That is why it
kept coming back: every invariant we had was satisfied.

Where the unbudgeted speech lands decides the symptom. At the end, the chapter merely looks
short. Anywhere earlier, everything after it is described by timings that are systematically
early, and the highlight leads the voice by a fixed few seconds.

**A second, independent bug, same symptom.** `tick()` gated on
`AVPlayer.timeControlStatus != .paused`, and `CADisplayLink.stop()` is terminal. `AVPlayer`
enters `.paused` transiently on its own — a stall while the resource loader serves a range,
a buffer hiccup, the gap between `play()` and the rate taking effect. One such frame froze
`currentTime` and `activeIndex` for the rest of the chapter while the audio played on. Now
gated on our own `isPlaying`. Needs a real device to reproduce; the simulator never stalls.

**Ruled out by measurement, so don't re-litigate:** the client-side fold
(`CharTokenMapper`), streaming concatenation, the `AVPlayer` clock (zero accumulating drift
even with a wrong advertised `contentLength`), the EPUB text itself, bracket handling
(four repeat generations showed brackets spoken and correctly budgeted), output latency
(speaker, no Bluetooth), and pronunciation dictionaries (alias matching is whitespace/word
bounded, so it cannot match inside CJK at all).

**Still unexplained.** The original report described the marker being wrong at the start of
a chapter and *converging* by the middle. An early unbudgeted insert produces a constant
offset, not a shrinking one. Nothing measured accounts for a convergence. If it recurs,
that is the thread to pull.

**Guard added.** `WorkerTTSService` now rejects a response whose audio and alignment
disagree by more than 1.0 s in either direction (`audioAlignmentMismatch`). A truncated or
over-spoken stream is internally consistent and would otherwise cache as a complete chapter.

## 2. Narration model: `eleven_v3` → `eleven_flash_v2_5`

Forced by §1: v3 cannot be used for a synced highlight at all, whatever its other merits.
This costs something real — v3 was chosen because it reads old orthography correctly
(生れ as うまれ, where multilingual_v2 says なまれ) — so the tradeoff is deliberate, not free.

**`ContentKey` includes the model**, so changing the default silently invalidated every
chapter the user had already paid for. `SynthesisRequest.cacheKeyCandidates` now probes
`SynthesisModel.previousDefaults` (`.v3`, `.multilingualV2`) before spending anything, and
`GeneratedAudioStore.loadAllowingLegacyModel` returns *which* key hit — a caller evicting a
corrupt entry has to evict the one it loaded, not the one it asked for.

Every cache-probing site has to agree or the badge lies about what will play offline:
`ReaderModel.load`, `AppServices.firstChapterKeys`, `LibraryModel`, `VoiceDemoPlayer`,
`refreshCachedChapters`.

## 3. Page OCR: `gemini-2.5-flash` → `openai/gpt-5.6-terra`

Full matrix, test pages and reproduction steps live in **`~/Projects/cloudflare/aiwork/OCR-MODELS.md`**.
Summary of what matters:

**We are still on Cloudflare AI Gateway.** The endpoint we ship —
`gateway.ai.cloudflare.com/v1/{acct}/{gw}/compat/chat/completions` — is now *deprecated* in
favour of `api.cloudflare.com/client/v4/accounts/{id}/ai/v1/chat/completions`. The old form
still works. The new one needs a token scoped to the whole `/ai/` namespace; a token with
only *AI Gateway → Run* gets 401 there (measured).

**Model availability is not what the catalog implies.** Probed live under Unified Billing:

| | |
|---|---|
| works | `openai/gpt-5.6-sol`, `gpt-5.6-terra`, `gpt-5.4`, `google-ai-studio/gemini-2.5-flash`, `gemini-2.5-pro` |
| 400 — not on Unified Billing | `gemini-3.6-flash`, `gemini-3.5-flash`, `gemini-3.1-pro` |
| 402 — wholesale rate limit, BYOK only | `anthropic/claude-opus-4.8` |

Gemini 3.6 Flash — the obvious "newer and cheaper" candidate — is **not reachable** without
adding a provider key, and is not cheaper anyway ($1.50/$7.50 per 1M against $0.30/$2.50).

**The finding that decided it.** `gemini-2.5-flash` transcribes Japanese book pages
*perfectly*: discount the page furniture it emits and its CER is **0.00 %**. Its entire
2.11 % error was the running header and page number it would not drop — deterministically,
3 runs of 3, on two different books.

That looks like a prompt problem. It is not. Four variants, two ground-truthed vertical
pages, three runs each:

| variant | page furniture | body-text fidelity |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | leaks 2 | intact |
| v2 — furniture as its own emphatic bullet | **0** | **deletes 「は外さないまま、」, 8 chars, 3/3** |
| v3 — folded into the existing bullet | leaks | intact |
| v4 — v2 plus an explicit ruby example | leaks 1 | intact |
| v5 — v2 plus "COMPLETENESS IS ABSOLUTE" | leaks | intact |

Every wording that suppressed the furniture cost fidelity; every wording that protected
fidelity leaked the furniture. **A dropped clause is worse than a leaked header** — a stray
title is visible and harmless, deleted prose is invisible, changes meaning, and is narrated
and highlighted as if correct.

`gpt-5.6-terra` does both with the prompt we already ship: 0.2 % CER, no furniture, and on
the second test page its output was 458 characters against 458 of truth — no deletion — with
one substitution (闇色→藍色, visually confusable kanji).

**Cost: ~$0.017/page against ~$0.001 — roughly $5.00 per 300-page book against $0.29.**
Bounded by the gateway's `cf-aig-cache-ttl` idempotency record and the client's page
checkpointing, so a book is normally OCR'd once.

**Reasoning models need a different request shape** — `max_completion_tokens`, no
`temperature`, and a far larger ceiling, because hidden reasoning bills against the same
budget. Confirmed live: a call capped at 10 tokens returned `finish_reason: "length"` with
`completion_tokens: 0` and no content at all. Encoded in `src/ocr.ts`.

The text-cleanup path stays on `gemini-2.5-flash` (`OCR_TEXT_MODEL`). It echoes its whole
input back, so output tokens scale with the document; a reasoning model there would make
"tidy this text" the most expensive call in the Worker.

**Worth revisiting when** Gemini 3.x reaches Unified Billing, or when page-furniture removal
moves into client code — a running header is *defined* by repeating across pages and a page
number is an isolated numeral, both detectable deterministically by the client, which sees
every page where the Worker sees one. That would let the cheap model back in, since
transcription alone is where it is already perfect.

## 4. Furigana from the book's own ruby

**Why the tokenizer is not enough.** MeCab + IPADic is reliable on ordinary words and does
not know proper nouns — which is exactly where a publisher bothers to print ruby. It reads
黄前 as きぜん, 希美 as きみ, drops the rendaku in 鎧塚, and could never produce
緑輝 → サファイア, which is a name rather than a reading. Import used to delete all 370 of
this book's annotations on the grounds that the reader generates its own furigana.

**Annotations are now carried** as `Chapter.sourceReadings` and overlaid on the tokens.
Offsets index the stored text and each annotation records the surface it was taken from, so
a range that no longer extracts that surface is dropped rather than trusted.

`Chapter` needed a hand-written `Codable`: Swift's synthesized decoder does **not** fall
back to a property's default when the key is absent, and `DiskLibraryStore` answers an
undecodable library by starting empty and moving the file aside. Adding the property without
one would have emptied every existing shelf on upgrade.

### 4.1 A book annotates a name once

| word | annotated | occurs |
|---|---|---|
| 久美子 | 1 | 972 |
| 希美 | 1 | 236 |
| 緑輝 | 1 | 99 |
| 黄前 | 1 | 3 |

Applying a reading only where the markup sits captures about **1 %** of what the book says,
and leaves the character-list page — which carries no ruby at all — reading きぜん for 黄前.
So readings are indexed book-wide and materialised at every occurrence, at import.

**370 → 6,494 readings.**

### 4.2 Single-character bases are excluded

Uniqueness among the annotations does not make a reading context-free, and for one kanji it
almost never is: a publisher rubies a lone kanji exactly where its reading is *unusual*.

| entry | would have been asserted over |
|---|---|
| 長 → た | 63 occurrences — the reading of 長ける, applied to 部長 and 長い |
| 微 → かす | 53 |
| 端 → ぱな | 44 — the rendaku'd 端から |
| 初 → しよ | 37 — applied over 初めて |
| 呆 → あき | 32 |
| 傾 → かし | 29 |

**64 single-char entries / 1,071 occurrences dropped; 142 multi-char entries / 2,494 kept** —
every name the exercise was for. A multi-kanji proper noun does not change reading with
context, which is why the publisher only annotated it once. **6,494 → 5,818 readings.**

Words the book itself reads two ways are excluded entirely rather than guessed at — one of
207 in this volume (労, both いたわ and ねぎら).

### 4.3 The source had flattened every small kana

This EPUB's ruby lost all its 小書き kana: **0 of 208 distinct readings contain one**, while
23 carry a sequence only a flattening could produce — 饒舌 written じようぜつ, 苗字 as
みようじ, 躊躇 as ちゆうちよ, サファイア as サフアイア. A property of the file, not typos.

It matters because the reading is asserted with the book's authority: it overrides the
tokenizer, drives the furigana, and is what TTS speaks. Left alone, import replaced MeCab's
correct じょうぜつ with a broken one — **worse than never having read the ruby**.

Detection needs the whole book (one chapter cannot tell a flattened file from a passage with
no small kana) and requires three things together: ≥12 readings, not one small kana among
them, and at least one 拗音-shaped sequence.

Restoration covers 拗音 and the フ/ヴ katakana digraphs. **Gemination is deliberately not
restored** — つ before a consonant is genuinely ambiguous (まつり is a word).

What makes restoring safe is that it is not final: where the book's reading and the
tokenizer's are the same word apart from small kana, **the tokenizer's spelling wins**
(`SourceReadingOverlay.preferred`). The comparison happens in the flattened space, so it
corrects a wrong restoration in *either* direction on every word MeCab knows. Only proper
nouns rely on the rule alone — 明静工科 みょうじょうこうか, 洛秋 らくしゅう, 大阪東照 とうしょう
are the places a bad guess could actually reach the page.

### 4.4 Ruby finer than the segmentation

The book writes 秀一 as two pairs (秀/しゆう, 一/いち) while MeCab keeps 秀一 whole and reads
it ひでかず. Neither annotation matched a token, so a character's name was read wrong. A run
of consecutive annotations that **tiles one token exactly** now concatenates onto it.

**Still deferred: the reverse.** One annotation spanning several tokens — 緑輝, one ruby base,
two MeCab tokens, read サファイア — is carried but not applied. Putting the reading on the
first token renders サファイア over 緑 and leaves 輝 bare; spreading one interval across
several tokens gives them all the same start, and `SpanTimeline.index(at:)` is a rightmost
search, so the earlier ones would never highlight. It needs range-based ruby in
`RubyTextView`. Tiling has no such problem: it writes one token's reading and moves no span.

## 5. The phantom silent tail

Surfaced only because §2 invalidated every cached chapter, sending all of them down the
progressive path.

**Symptom.** Audio stopped before the text ended; the timer and the highlight kept running
with no sound.

**Not the audio.** The cached file was 277,987 bytes / 17.37 s with speech continuous to
17.11 s, and its alignment ended at 17.32 s — agreeing to 0.05 s. Confirmed by listening.

**The cause.** `AVAssetResourceLoader` asks for an asset's length **exactly once**, when the
item is attached — long before a generated chapter's length is known. `ChapterAudioSource`
answers with an estimate that deliberately runs long, because advertising short truncates
playback outright. `finish()` corrects the stored value, but nothing re-asks.

```
80 chars ÷ charsPerSecondOfSpeech 3.5  = 22.9 s
22.9 s × bytesPerSecondOfAudio 20,000  = 457,143 bytes advertised   (28.6 s)
real audio                             = 277,987 bytes             (17.4 s)
                                       → ~11 s of silence, clock still running
```

`didPlayToEndTime` does not fire until that phantom tail elapses. Cached chapters never
showed it — `buildPlayback` passes the exact byte count.

**Fix.** Sealing installs a boundary time observer at the true duration. A boundary observer
rather than the display link, because the link is a foreground-only clock and a chapter has
to end correctly while backgrounded; removed on teardown and when a player is replaced,
since a stale one would end the *next* chapter at the previous one's length.

The over-estimate itself stays — advertising short is the worse failure. No test covers
this: a boundary observer needs a real player and a real asset. Verified by hand.

## Measurement tooling

Keep these; every conclusion above came out of one of them.

| tool | answers |
|---|---|
| `scripts/eleven-matrix.mjs` | does a TTS model's alignment actually describe its audio? (model × route × params, `--forced-alignment`, `--estimate`) |

| `aiwork/scripts/make-vertical-page.mjs` | builds a 縦書き page with ruby, running header and page number **plus exact ground truth** — the only pages that discriminate OCR models |
| `app/ReaderTests/RealBookRubyProbe.swift` | env-gated (`YOMI_EPUB`); prints every reading override and where in the book to look at it |
| `ffmpeg -af silencedetect` | is a chapter's tail silent, or absent? |

**Horizontal pages with no page furniture separate nothing** — every OCR model scored ~0 %
on them. Only the adversarial vertical pages discriminate.

## Mistakes worth remembering

Each of these was stated confidently and was wrong:

- **"The mapper absorbs it cleanly."** Claimed from a probe that only asserted non-NaN and
  monotonicity while `print`ing the semantic checks. It proved nothing.
- **"Gemini hallucinated the character list."** Disproved by a screenshot of the actual page.
  The OCR was fine; the wrong volume was being read.
- **"Unaligned bracket audio causes the drift."** Four repeat generations showed brackets
  spoken and correctly budgeted.
- **"Ship the v2 OCR prompt."** It fixed page furniture by silently deleting body text — a
  strictly worse failure, caught only by testing a *second* book.
- **"The token only needs AI Gateway → Run."** True for the deprecated endpoint, 401 on the
  new REST API.

The pattern: the measurement that overturned each one was cheap, and running it first would
have cost less than the retraction.
